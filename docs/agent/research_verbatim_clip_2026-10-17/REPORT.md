# Serve one verbatim clip per facet

**Evidence caveat first:** the underlying research could not open most primary papers (arxiv.org, aclanthology.org, qdrant.tech, nngroup.com and others failed with DNS errors or proxy 403s). Nearly every external claim below comes from web-search result summaries of the named source, not from full text. Items read in full were Anthropic's contextual-retrieval post and cookbook, the Qdrant OpenAPI JSON, and the Jina, docTTTTTquery and FlagEmbedding READMEs. Claims marked UNVERIFIED rest on a single secondary source or on my reasoning. Figures labelled "project" come from the repository's own CLAUDE.md, not from a public source. No source found tests this exact product (verbatim spoken-teacher clips as the answer), so every recommendation is assembled from adjacent work and must be validated on the project's own gold sets.

The five recommendations point the same way: keep the served words untouched, and put all intelligence into choosing boundaries, indexing signals that are never shown, and the order and parallelism of gates. (1) Treat a compound question as several independent questions: split it once, retrieve and judge each facet in parallel, serve at most one clip per facet, and say "no recording for this part" when a facet fails its gate. (2) Repair clips that open on "this" or "yes, these" by moving the start back to an earlier sentence boundary, never by rewriting, and skip the clip when no clean start exists. (3) Put contextual signals in a separate vector or in payload, not in the embedded passage, and decide this before the calibration batch is labelled, because any change to embedded text or fusion invalidates a fitted threshold. (4) Start the question-only answerability gate at t=0 beside retrieval, score the top clips as concurrent calls, and add a cross-encoder only as a shadow-mode, recall-tuned pre-filter. (5) Present each clip as a quote card with an always-visible attribution line, our own labels in a visibly different register, and one answer-level honesty line.

| # | Do | Expected effect | Primary measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Split compound questions, one clip per facet, explicit "no recording" | Facet coverage up without raising wrong-clip rate | Facet coverage and per-facet precision on a compound gold set |
| 2 | Boundary-only repair of orphan openers | Fewer clips that need a missing sentence | Orphan-opener rate before/after, human "stands alone" rating, skip rate |
| 3 | Context in a separate vector or payload; new collection | Recall gain without silently moving the gate score | Recall@k, top-1 score distributions, risk-coverage per arm |
| 4 | Gate parallel to retrieval; concurrent scoring; shadow cross-encoder | Wall-clock moves from a sum toward a max of the stages | p50/p95, precision non-inferiority on served clips |
| 5 | Quote cards plus labelled framing | Clear provenance, less "AI-written" feel | Attribution recall, mixed-register errors, rater comprehension |

## Compound questions need fan-out, per-facet gates, and honest gaps

"How can meditation heal suffering and imbibe love, prosperity and success?" is a parallel multi-facet question, not a multi-hop chain, so the right pattern is fan-out: decompose, retrieve per facet, merge, rerank. Sequential methods such as least-to-most prompting feed each answer into the next step ([Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.10625)) and suit multi-hop problems, which this is not. The closest retrieval result is Ammann, Golde and Akbik (ACL 2025 SRW): an LLM splits the question, passages are retrieved per sub-question, and a cross-encoder reranks the merged pool, reported at **MRR@10 +36.7% and answer F1 +11.6%** over standard RAG ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-srw.32)). A third-party summary frames the same work as +16.5% Hits@10 on MultiHop-RAG ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2507.00355v1)), so the exact gain is UNVERIFIED, and the first figure looks like a relative gain with no absolute values seen. LlamaIndex's `SubQuestionQueryEngine` implements the same split-answer-synthesize shape, though our "synthesize" step must be replaced by clip selection ([docs](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/python/examples/query_engine/sub_question_query_engine/index.md)).

Recommended design: a cheap detector (two or more conjunctions or commas, or length) flags likely compounds; only those get one small, non-reasoning model call that returns a JSON list of at most 3 to 4 facets, each rewritten to carry the shared head ("meditation" plus the facet) so retrieval keeps the topic. Rule-based splitting alone is free but will split "peace and quiet" or "body and mind"; no source tested this, so it is a hypothesis to check on the gold set. Then run retrieval, the answerability gate and relevance scoring for every facet in parallel, take the top clip per facet, and drop any clip whose time range overlaps or is contained in another accepted clip from the same video (project: 27% of v8 clips are contained in another clip). A clip that best answers two facets is served once and labelled for both. MMR alone does not guarantee facet coverage, since it spreads results in embedding space without ensuring each sub-query has a clip ([Carbonell and Goldstein, SIGIR 1998](https://people.eng.unimelb.edu.au/ammoffat/sigir98/abstracts/carbonell.html); [diversification survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14464)). Facet-aware diversification such as xQuAD is the textbook alternative, but the research could not verify its details (UNVERIFIED).

The key risk is multiplied decisions. Each (facet, clip) pair is its own accept-or-abstain call, so gold labels for calibration are counted in pairs, and a union-bound argument (my arithmetic, not a sourced result) says end-to-end error can grow roughly with the number of served facets. Until a calibrated profile exists, serve every facet in the project's current "Related, not a direct answer" state, or say "no recording for this part." Raw model probabilities are poor abstention signals out of domain; a trained calibrator answered 56% of questions at 80% accuracy versus 48% for softmax confidence in Kamath, Jia and Liang ([arXiv 2006.09462](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09462)), which supports the project's rule against hand-picked thresholds. The cost is roughly one extra model round trip plus N parallel calls; the project measured 4 to 6 s uncached for the current path and merged independent LLM calls into one `asyncio.gather` to cut comparative-query p95 from 165.4 s to 113.0 s. The latency of a decomposer call was not measured, so measure before shipping.

To measure, build a small human-labelled set of compound questions and report **facet coverage** (facets with a correct clip served) and **per-facet precision** as separate numbers, following the partial-credit idea in MultiSpanQA ([Li et al., NAACL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.90)), plus p95 latency with and without decomposition, and the share of requests that trigger the decomposer. Expected effect: higher coverage on compound questions at unchanged per-clip precision; no published number transfers.

## Orphan openers are fixed by moving boundaries, never by rewriting

The literature on making passages self-contained is almost entirely rewriting: Dense X Retrieval's propositions resolve "the tower" to "Pisa Tower" ([arXiv 2312.06648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)), decontextualization edits sentences until they stand alone ([Choi et al., TACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.27)), and CLAP rewrites pronouns and reports up to 20.68 absolute nDCG@10 on BEIR/MS MARCO ([arXiv 2508.06941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06941)). None is usable for served text. What transfers is the diagnosis. Sentence-initial demonstratives ("this", "these") usually point to a clause or whole prior sentence rather than a noun phrase, which entity-based coreference models handle poorly ([Artstein and Poesio](https://publishup.uni-potsdam.de/files/947/artstein_poesio.pdf)). A lexical rule is therefore likely more reliable than a coreference model for the opener, with a model such as Maverick ([ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.722)) as a secondary check for entity mentions. This is my inference; no source measured it on ASR text.

Recommended repair, boundary-only by construction: detect an opener (after stripping "yes", "so", "and", "now", "well") that is a demonstrative or personal pronoun, or a dependent connective like "but" or "because", plus a trailing conjunction at the end; move the start back one Whisper sentence boundary; re-check, because the new first sentence may itself be an orphan; stop at a speaker change, a topic boundary, or a length cap; if no clean start exists, skip the clip. The speaker stop is essential: the project already found that long pre-roll leaks host speech (host voice is 6.9% of top-1 clips), and extending backward toward a host question is the main hazard. Topic segmenters such as TextTiling or Koshorek's supervised model ([NAACL 2018](https://aclanthology.org/N18-2075/)) work at minute scale and are best used as a coarse "do not cross" constraint, not as the boundary generator. Length matters too: Dense X found the largest gain over passages at about 100 to 200 words ([arXiv 2312.06648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)), which at an assumed 120 to 160 words per minute is roughly 45 to 90 s. That conversion is my assumption, and no controlled study of spoken-clip length and comprehension exists, so set the length cap empirically and track skip rate against it.

Measure with proxies validated by humans: orphan-opener rate, dangling-connective rate, and a cross-boundary-reference rate in the spirit of the "References Completeness" metric ([arXiv 2603.25333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25333)); then a two-rater "stands alone / needs preceding sentence / no" label on a stratified sample, using the yes/no/title protocol from decontextualization work ([Choi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.27)) to estimate the detector's own precision and recall. Track repair side effects (skip rate, length change, host-leak rate) and keep an unrepaired control group. Topic-boundary metrics (Pk, WindowDiff) are the wrong yardstick because they cannot detect an orphan "this" ([Pevzner and Hearst 2002](https://aclanthology.org/J02-1002/)). No benchmark or base rate for orphan openers in sentence-segmented talks exists; measure it on the live collection first.

## Keep context out of the passage vector, and decide before calibrating

Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval prepends a 50 to 100 token generated context to each chunk before both embedding and BM25 indexing, and reports top-20 retrieval failure falling from 5.7% to 3.7% (**-35%**), 2.9% with contextual BM25 (**-49%**), and 1.9% with reranking (**-67%**) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval)). These are Anthropic's own benchmarks on English codebases, fiction and papers with roughly 800-token chunks; I found only vendor reproductions, not independent ones, and the one academic comparison reports a mixed picture (better coherence, higher compute cost; full text unread, UNVERIFIED) ([arXiv 2504.19754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19754)). Do not transfer those percentages to short spoken clips. Critically, Anthropic's method is the "prepend" design, so none of its numbers speak to the metadata or separate-vector designs.

The better-matched evidence supports separating the signal. Doc2Query++ reports that appending generated queries injects noise into dense embeddings, and that encoding text and generated queries separately and fusing with a weight (Dual-Index Fusion) beats appending, with nDCG@10 peaking at fusion weights around 0.3 to 0.6 ([arXiv 2510.09557](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09557); search summary only). Unfiltered generated queries can also hurt ([Doc2Query--](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.03266)), which fits the project's rule that offline LLM output passes a review gate before any Qdrant write. Qdrant's Query API supports exactly this layout: named vectors per point, one prefetch per vector via `using`, outer RRF or DBSF fusion, and RRF `weights` and `k` in the current spec ([OpenAPI](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qdrant/qdrant/master/docs/redoc/master/openapi.json); the minimum server version for weights is UNVERIFIED). The existing verbatim text, sha256 hash and uuid5 point ids stay untouched because the hash covers only verbatim text.

The calibration consequence is the strongest argument for deciding now. Contextualization shifts similarity unevenly: in Jina's late-chunking example, a chunk that already names Berlin barely moves (0.8486 to 0.8495) while anaphoric chunks jump from 0.7084 to 0.8249 ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jina-ai/late-chunking/main/README.md)); that is a different mechanism from prepending, so it is an analogue only. Learn-then-Test style guarantees hold for the exact scoring pipeline that was calibrated, under exchangeability ([Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01052)), so embedded text, vectors, fusion type, `k`, weights and reranker are all part of "the threshold." With three lists, the RRF maximum rises from 2/(k+1) to 3/(k+1) (my arithmetic), so a threshold on the fused score is not portable when a prefetch is added. Since the project has no fitted profile (14 human labels against 299 needed), a re-index costs nothing now and would force new labels later. Recommended ordering by drift: payload-only metadata, then a separate context vector used only to generate candidates while the final gate keeps using the original score or the reranker, then context inside the RRF, then prepending. Build the candidate as a new collection and run both against a frozen question set, covering recall@1/3/10, MRR, nDCG@10 with bootstrap intervals, top-1 score quantiles and KS distance for correct, wrong and out-of-corpus queries, risk-coverage curves, out-of-corpus leaks (target 0) and latency. The protocol is synthesis, not a published standard; with about 100 questions, differences of a few points are noise.

## Start the gate at t=0 and let a cross-encoder earn its place in shadow mode

The answerability gate reads only the question, so it has no dependency on retrieval. Running a guardrail concurrently with the main work and discarding the result if the guardrail fires is documented practice ([OpenAI cookbook](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/how_to_use_guardrails); [NeMo Guardrails](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/guardrails/latest/configure-guardrails/yaml-schema/guardrails-configuration)), though NeMo warns of races when rails mutate input and recommends it for I/O-bound rails. No published source gives percentage savings. Expected effect (inference): wall-clock approaches the longest stage instead of the sum, saving up to the gate's 6 s budget minus retrieval time. Rules to keep: crisis and topic rails run first or concurrently with nothing speculative served or logged until they clear; cancel in-flight scorer calls when the gate says NO; score the top 3 (question, clip) pairs as separate concurrent calls, not one reasoning call; and keep using `run_llm_coroutine`, not `asyncio.run()`, as the project requires. Speculation spends tokens on abstained questions, so track cost per request.

Should a cross-encoder replace the LLM scorer? Not yet. The evidence says LLM judges track humans well at the system level (Kendall tau about 0.86 to 0.94, relayed second-hand from UMBRELA via a [survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12558v1)) but that is not per-clip precision, and the same survey reports that the LLM labelled 26% more documents perfectly relevant than humans did, a leniency risk for a precision-first gate. Prompt wording also changes accuracy ([Thomas et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/large-language-models-can-accurately-predict-searcher-preferences/)), and using one LLM as both serving component and judge is circular ([Clarke and Dietz](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17156v2)), so keep the evaluation judge from a different model family, as the project already does with an independent Opus judge. Smaller judges degrade ([Farzi and Dietz](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09483v1)), though splitting a binary decision from a graded one narrowed the gap, with a reported +18.4% Krippendorff's alpha over GPT-4o mini on TREC DL only and no independent replication ([Schnabel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14296v1)). Cross-encoder scores are rank-correct but not portable probabilities: BAAI shows a sigmoid mapping that changes scale only ([bge-reranker-v2-m3](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3)), and Cohere advises choosing thresholds empirically ([docs](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/reranking-best-practices)). A reranker-specific abstention method exists ([arXiv 2402.12997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12997); results unread). Latency evidence is informal: about 0.35 s on CPU and 0.08 s on a T4 for 3 documents in one blog test ([Medium](https://medium.com/@xiweizhou/speed-showdown-reranker-1f7987400077)); clip-length inputs on this host are unmeasured. A cross-encoder also scores topical relevance, not "complete standalone answer," which the 0-3 rubric encodes.

Recommended path: first the parallelism changes, which cost no accuracy; then a cascade in which the project's ONNX INT8 reranker removes obvious rejects at a threshold tuned for high recall of true score-3 clips while the strong LLM keeps the final say, which saves time only on the reject path; then, only if shadow mode shows precision non-inferior on held-out human-anchored labels, anything stronger. Measure with a paired replay of one fixed question set (McNemar on show/don't-show), a risk-coverage view with threshold chosen on one split and reported on another ([arXiv 2607.06799](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06799)), and Clopper-Pearson intervals. The project's last "don't show" figure of 12% rests on 8 served clips and is statistically uninformative; set a minimum served-item count per arm, enlarge the 13-item out-of-corpus set, and treat coverage losses as acceptable but precision losses as not. The non-inferiority margin is the owner's decision.

## Present quotes as cards, with our own words visibly ours

No verified precedent exists to copy. Public documentation for Ask Sadhguru and Delphi says nothing about answer layout or timestamps (Delphi says only that answers "include references" ([embed.delphi.ai](https://embed.delphi.ai/))), and no product was found that interleaves verbatim clips with labelled AI framing. Hands-on inspection of those products is needed (UNVERIFIED). The reusable verified building blocks are small. Google's Clip markup pairs a human-readable label with start and end offsets ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/data-types/video)), and YouTube chapters follow the same label-plus-time pattern with a 10-second minimum chapter length that is a platform rule, not a clip-length target ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579?hl=en)). Perplexity attaches numbered sources per answer section and carries a standing "may contain mistakes" notice ([Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352895-how-does-perplexity-work)).

Trust research argues for care. Reference links raised trust in generative AI even when incorrect, while highlighting uncertainty lowered willingness to trust and share ([arXiv 2504.06435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435v1)); an audit of four generative search engines found only 74.5% of citations supported their sentence ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)). A visible attribution is only honest if the clip really answers the question, which is why the "Related, not a direct answer" label must stay. Hover cards supported on-demand verification while an aligned sidebar helped under high citation density ([arXiv 2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14611v1)); the study did not test clip UIs, so on mobile an always-visible attribution line is my inference. I found no study comparing verbatim quotation with paraphrase for comprehension or trust; that is a gap, not a finding. Scanning guidance from NN/g (79% of users always scan; meaningful subheadings, front-loaded keywords, one idea per paragraph, numbers only when order matters) is from a 1997 study reconfirmed later and read via summaries ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/); [bulleted lists](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/presenting-bulleted-lists/)).

Recommended layout (design proposal, not an established standard): one sentence of our own lead-in stating what the clips cover, in plain body text with a fixed small tag such as "Guide note"; then one block-quote card per clip, led by a short bold topic label we write, containing only the unedited transcript, with a single consistent attribution line (speaker, video title, start time, play control); no numbering unless the clips are genuinely ordered; for compound questions, one card per facet, and a "No recording for this part" card for a failed facet; and a single answer-level honesty line instead of hedging each card, to limit the uncertainty-trust penalty. Never mix registers within a sentence, never write framing in the teachers' first person, and avoid transitions that fuse clips into one flowing paragraph, which I hypothesize is what makes multi-clip answers feel stitched and AI-written (UNVERIFIED; A/B test it). Measure attribution recall (can a rater name speaker and source after reading), mixed-register errors in a rater audit, comprehension and perceived-AI-authorship ratings in an A/B of card layout against flowing text, and click-through to the play control.

## Conclusion

The common thread is that the verbatim constraint turns every "make the passage better" technique into a "choose a different boundary, index signal or gate order" technique. Rewriting methods (propositions, pronoun resolution, decontextualization) supply the diagnosis, and only their index-time, never-displayed variants are usable. The most time-sensitive decision is not a modeling choice but a sequencing one: the contextual-indexing design must be settled before the 299-pair calibration batch is labelled, because the calibration guarantee belongs to the whole scoring pipeline, and compound questions multiply the number of pairs that need labels.

What would change these recommendations is measurement the literature cannot supply: the base rate of orphan openers, the decomposer's real latency, whether a recall-tuned cross-encoder keeps precision at the LLM scorer's level, and whether quote cards beat flowing text with users. Treat the percentages cited here as direction-setting from other domains, not forecasts, and run each recommendation as a shadow-mode A/B on a frozen human-labelled set with intervals reported.
