# The Power Of Observation In Hindu Beliefs

## Verbatim Discourse Excerpts
Namaste. I have seen people all over the world marvel at India and the vibrant Hindu culture. We are the only surfing ancient civilization among 32 ancient civilizations that flourished on earth since the dawn of history as we know it. Hinduism has survived every challenge. It survives in its festivals, it survives in its gods, its traditions and above all its most unique and undrivaled philosophy that has populated even to the least educated person in Indian society.

The Hindu mind is a very ancient mind. It is not a gross mind that is built on historic facts. It is a mind that runs on principles, on the spirit of things. It is a mind that is capable of subtleties. To the Hindu it is not important to know if Rama was born this particular day, at this particular time or this particular year. It is of no consequence to the Hindu. Whether Sri Rama was born 7,000 years ago or 50,000 years ago or 20 lakh years ago, that a Mahapurisham like Sri Rama did walk the earth offers immense spiritual strength and offers meaning to the Hindu mind because for a Hindu the Mahapurisham Sri Rama is real. The historicity of the person of Rama or the accuracy of the size of the footprint found in any sacred place of worship is unimportant to the Hindu mind. The Hindu mind does not think whether that footprint was made by a sculptor or whether it was an imprint made by Rama's actual feet. The name of Rama and the identity of that footprint with Sri Rama is enough to worship it. You tell the Hindu Rama is blue, he is fine. You tell a Hindu that Rama is cold and in complexion, he is fine too. The Hindu mind worships the being of Rama.
