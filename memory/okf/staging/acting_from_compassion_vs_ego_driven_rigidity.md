# Acting from Compassion vs. Ego-Driven Rigidity

## Summary
This teaching uses the story of Confucius to illustrate the spiritual danger of acting from rigid ideals rather than genuine connection. It emphasizes that true spiritual action emerges from present-moment intelligence and compassion, not from performative righteousness or adherence to ego-driven principles.

## Key Teachings
- Acting from rigid ideals, such as obsessive honesty, can disconnect us from genuine relationships and the larger context of life. (Unknown speaker)
- True spiritual action arises from compassion and present-moment intelligence, not from adherence to performative righteousness. (Unknown speaker)

## Quotes
> "Consequently, Confucius jailed the boy—not the father—to demonstrate that true spiritual action emerges from present-moment intelligence and compassion, not from performative righteousness or adherence to rigid ideals." — Unknown speaker

## Related Concepts
- Ego-driven action: Acting from rigid principles or performative righteousness, often disconnected from compassion and context.
- Compassionate action: Acting from present-moment intelligence and genuine connection, prioritizing relationships and context over rigid ideals.
