# The Consequences of Repression

## Summary
This teaching addresses the question of whether to ignore internal struggles like depression while maintaining external functioning. It argues that suppression and repression worsen mental illness by covering up underlying wounds, refuting the idea that ignoring inner pain leads to healing. The core thesis is that internal states must be acknowledged rather than repressed to achieve true well-being.

## Key Teachings
- Attempting to ignore internal struggles like depression while maintaining external functioning is a form of suppression that worsens the underlying condition.
- Repression acts as a cover-up that prevents the underlying wounds from being addressed, leading to the worsening of mental illness.
- The idea that ignoring inner pain leads to healing is fundamentally flawed; true healing requires confronting and resolving internal states rather than suppressing them.
