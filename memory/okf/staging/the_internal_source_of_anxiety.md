# The Internal Source of Anxiety

## Summary
Anxiety and stress are often mistakenly believed to stem from external problems. However, the true source of anxiety is frequently internal, arising from self-focused thoughts and concerns. Dwelling on these thoughts, which center on "me, me," can lead to significant stress due to worries about personal outcomes.

## Key Teachings
- The common misconception is that anxiety and stress originate from problems ("The common misconception that anxiety and stress stem from problems is often a source of confusion and stress.")
- The key to alleviating anxiety and stress is recognizing that the source is often internal, specifically self-focused thoughts and concerns ("The key to alleviating anxiety and stress is recognizing that the source of anxiety is often the internal, self-focused thoughts and concerns.")
- Focusing on these self-centered thoughts, which are about "me, me," can cause tremendous stress due to worries about what will happen to oneself ("By focusing on these thoughts, one is going over a thinking process that is all about 'me, me.' This can lead to tremendous stress because one is concerned about what's going to happen to them.")

- Stressful State: a condition characterized by feelings of anxiety, tension, or overwhelm.
