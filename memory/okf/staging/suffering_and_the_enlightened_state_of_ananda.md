# Suffering and the Enlightened State of Ananda

## Summary
The enlightened state of Ananda is characterized by inherent bliss in every experience, including mental and sensory ones. This state signifies complete wholeness and an intoxication with bliss, where consciousness is untouched by suffering. Suffering, in contrast, arises from holding onto judgments and labels, leading to separation and pain, and is not the true nature of consciousness.

## Key Teachings
- In the enlightened state of Ananda, every experience, including mental and sensory ones, is inherently blissful. (Unknown speaker)
- The experience of bliss in all aspects of consciousness is a state of complete wholeness, where one is intoxicated with bliss. (Unknown speaker)
- Suffering is a state that affects consciousness and is not its true nature. (Unknown speaker)
- Suffering arises from holding onto judgments and labels, leading to separation and pain. (Unknown speaker)

## Related Concepts
- Ananda: An enlightened state where every experience, including mental and sensory ones, is inherently blissful, characterized by complete wholeness and intoxication with bliss.
- Judgment: A label or perception that can lead to suffering and separation, and is a root cause of a complaining mind.
- Suffering: A state of unhappiness and pain that can be experienced as emotional or physical pain, arising from holding onto judgments and labels, and is not the true nature of consciousness.
